## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Internal exposure dominates the pre-stage opportunity.** Making the pre-stage noiseless lowers total loss from 7.13059568e-5 to 5.12292461e-5 (−28.15%); its net cubic term drops from 1.69860290e-5 to 3.68176464e-6 (−78.32%). Removing only internal pre-stage idles gives 5.41148770e-5 (−24.11%). Pre-stage-only loss is 3.93363873e-6; the remaining 1.61430720e-5 of its full-vs-no-pre effect is cross-region interaction, including normalization. [Ablation](ablation.json).
- **Logical Z triples through long data waits.** The largest explicit triple is Z on q10 at steps 31–44, q3 at 28–43, and q0 at 30–43, contributing 1.13019773e-7. These are code positions {3,4,0}, a weight-three logical-Z support. q10 is a hop-owned idle; q0/q3 are pre-owned. Raw triples containing an internal pre idle sum to 1.25480688e-5 (59.1% of raw weight-three mass). The raw 2.12287688e-5 triple sum and −4.24273979e-6 pair-times-others term must be combined to obtain the cubic term. [Complete grouped sets](decomposition.json).
- **A faults combine with old data errors.** A×pre pairs contribute 4.12194362e-6; 3.13083992e-6 of that pairs A with internal pre idles. A×hop adds 2.75187758e-6. Several top pairs combine a q10 Z idle with ZZ/ZI/XY/IZ errors in the final A-encoder CXs; each displayed aggregated-channel pair contributes 3.08429142e-7. This supports the mechanism of correlated A Z errors completing a logical support with one surviving data error. Pair attribution is measured; this propagation description is an inference from the exact fault signatures.
- **The encoder alone has little intrinsic second-order opportunity.** No harmful pair in the base decomposition involves an injection-encoder channel; making those channels noiseless changes only the cubic term, lowering total by 5.59471688e-7. Substituting 21 CX instead of 19 helps the check combination by advancing its schedule, while the 21-CX encoder alone does not beat the base on the measured MC mean.
- **The new check must retain its fault detection.** A blue X stabilizer check with the Bell uncompute passes and helps. Omitting the unbell CX, then taking parity of both ancilla readouts, changes the blue-X exact loss from 7.03407384e-5 to 9.56469790e-5, mainly through the second-order term (5.35873985e-5 to 7.82738108e-5). The saved CX costs more in undetected pairs than it saves in noise. [Unbell-free receipt](cat_1_x_0_0.exact.json).
- **Acceptance trades are real circuit changes.** Full H_XY repetition (24 added CX) and full stabilizer rounds extend R to 55–58 and fail acceptance. Smaller blue X/XZ checks were rebuilt with each encoder, reversed Bell orientation, cat/A state reuse, and selected optional suffix checks removed. Every trade is separately scored in the table; acceptance is not an objective.
- **Compression is not equivalent to removing idle noise.** Minimum pre-gap schedules recover acceptance but move exposure into later data/block lifetimes. The fixed-suffix compact control is 7.58885274e-5 despite higher acceptance 0.55563; the ablation ceiling cannot be claimed as an implementable gain.
- **Corrected failure mechanisms.** A late q13 flag initially overwrote an active BC state; the reserved-lifetime variant delays BC and was re-evaluated. Bare-cat individual MX results are random even when their parity is fixed; q10 must be reset after the last native measurement before its |+>-based data hop. The corrected postreset recipes pass noiseless correctness but lose on fidelity/acceptance. A Z-measurement replacing a reused injection reset is not a deterministic preparation and fails correctness. These failures do not exclude other flags or measurement-based designs.

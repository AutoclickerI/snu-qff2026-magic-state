# Phase-aware detector and native herald search

This is a bounded search, not a new architecture or a SOTA claim. The existing complete native late-phase reference has exact L=3.5681272391347155e-5 and A=0.501016046737387. The 120 most frequent benign single-fault detector sectors were screened with their extra pair risks, re-derived against the actual late-phase circuit using the validated lane-local Clifford frontend. The most promising sector, `accept_cand/one_0`, has exact L=3.568127239157278e-5 and A=0.5046927063337008; the change in loss is numerical noise while acceptance increases.

Four deterministic, physical, noisy idle-slot measurements were tested individually and in combinations on that relaxed circuit. The run writes each complete raw-checked candidate and its exact results to `heralds/` and `heralds.json`. The currently best single read is MX14@35: L=3.56787924983954e-5, A=0.5041873166908268. These are tiny changes and do not address the requested 10% target. Every measurement adds its real noise; the original clock is preserved.

An official pinned 1M-shot check on the M13@34 variant passed all gates with A=0.503816, 412 CX, sampled L=4.76364387e-5 with SEM=8.3e-6 (`official_1m.log`). The sample is too noisy to rank the candidates. The all-orders pricing uses the same `axis_echo.exact` validated phase frontend and pinned contraction as the input. `acceptance_search.py` is a lane-local adaptation; the full pricing of six further sectors was interrupted after they all increased L, and the 120 screens remain in `search.log`. `herald_test.py` is the complete-circuit generator and all-orders scorer. At this checkpoint its 15-case combination run may still be in progress; the JSON ledger is authoritative for completed cases.

The full 15-case herald combination run completed. Best eligible is M13@34, M33@39, MX14@35: L=3.56764692613793e-5, A=0.5020412914487257, a 0.01346% improvement. Candidate stem: `heralds/h13t34_33t39_14t35_b35c099d591c3662_d6_20_19_10_2_22_16`. All four reads give L=3.5676250091923186e-5 but A=0.49983981458511245, so fail acceptance. Each of the 15 candidates passed raw correctness; only the earlier M13-only case has an official 1M check. This finite exploration does not rule out a different correlated detector or native check construction.

## Joint affine-frame recovery

`frame_search.py` screens actual native fault sectors and all three nontrivial whole-code affine Pauli corrections. It constructs each check from the actual detector record basis, so the frame selector vanishes on the noiseless branch. 65 sector/correction combinations had negligible harmful single-fault contribution and a first-order acceptance coefficient above 0.05. The lowest screened added pair-risk per unit acceptance coefficient was 3.6. The approximate break-even ratio against the reference loss at p=0.001 is 0.0357; this motivates but does not itself replace complete finite-p contraction.

The lane-local frame-aware contraction exactly reproduced the no-frame phase reference: L=3.5681272391347155e-5, A=0.501016046737387. The three best pair-ratio framed sectors received all-orders prices. All are raw correct, but their losses are 4.288086923710359e-5, 4.415464199764482e-5 and 5.318256891552759e-5. The first has A=0.502019080860947 and uses `FRAME_X(0) c[338]`; the official 1M judge check passes all gates (sampled loss 5.28807e-5 ±8.41e-6; A=0.501128). These are not eligible improvements and the sample is too noisy for ranking. The 12-case full-price loop was interrupted after the first three; `frame_screen.json` contains all 65 pair screens and `frame_prices.json` records the complete prices. This is a bounded screen of observed single-fault sectors, not a proof against arbitrary multi-sector or alternative detector/check architectures.

`frame_pair_audit.json` decomposes the best framed sector's added 7.2 pair coefficient. Most of it pairs reset/read faults on q23 at clocks 29–30 with the final data-idle channels represented on the clock-47 CX operations. This is why an otherwise first-order-safe recovered sector is expensive in the full circuit.

A distinct two-direction candidate combines the previously benign unframed acceptance sector with the best X-framed sector (`frame_cand/joint0`). The affine selector is constrained to vanish on the first direction and act on the second. Its raw output is valid, and the exhaustive single-fault audit has zero harmful faults (`frame_joint_singles.json`). The official 1M check passes with sampled A=0.504765, sampled loss 5.24997e-5 ±8.35e-6 and 412 CX; this does not determine its mean. Its extra cross-sector pair coefficient is zero (`frame_joint_cross_pairs.json`), but the costly 7.2 framed-sector contribution remains. A complete all-orders contraction was launched in `frame_joint.py`; inspect `frame_joint.log` for a completed result rather than treating the pair estimate as exact.

## SpiderCSS constructive resource and full-noise controls

The source is the public compiler [SpiderCSS](https://github.com/boldar99/spidercss), corresponding to [arXiv:2610.03714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03714). Its compiler and public dependencies were retrieved into isolated directories, without changing the shared `.venv`. `run_spidercss.py` generated a 15-CX/3-flag Steane-zero resource. Its exact output permutation was verified with Stim (`spider_map.py`). A complete *abstract*, fully noisy bell2/source and B-only ending with this preparation passed raw and singles but cost L=1.361605449113419e-4, A=0.7763311859527634 at 20 clocks. This is not a native result; its single-clock source delay and missing C verification make its comparison conditional on this layout.

More relevantly, `spider_bc.py` passed the corrected six-square B/C target's seven X generators to the compiler. It produced a 30-CX, 6-flag, 20-qubit preparation in `spider_bc.log`, with outputs 0..13 in B then C coordinate order. `spider_bc_check.py` postselected the six measurements and checked the seven target X generators and all 128 Z strings in the dual orthogonal space; all expectations are +1. This checks the complete pure CSS state, but does not assert useful noisy residue or native embeddability.

`spider_bc_abstract.py` composes that literal resource, both original source checks, both physical ending interactions, six-square C-to-A checks, and all actual resets, reads and idles into a full abstract circuit. The official exact raw output passes and exhaustive singles give c1=0. The first complete 27-clock schedule has L=4.53974311212649e-5, A=0.6309709427220075; flag remapping evaluated 720 assignments and found resource ready one clock earlier. The resulting complete 26-clock circuit prices L=4.233677918491872e-5, A=0.6433397597335433 (125 CX). It is nonnative and worse than the 19-clock abstract square control near 3.0094e-5. Source-cat reuse causes the resource to start late; different native co-scheduling remains a research question. Exact receipt: `spider_bc_abstract_result.json`.

The 10M official judge sample of the two-direction framed candidate completed: 1-F=4.2249318074e-5 with SEM 2.43e-6 and A=0.5053336. All gates pass. Its all-orders evaluation was interrupted after an extended contraction when the work budget was redirected to SpiderCSS; it has no exact joint receipt.

## Four-square mask 85 and scheduling

The mask-85 B/C target has 3 joint X masks [85,102,120] and 4 C-only masks [85,2,8,32]. SpiderCSS generated a 24-CX, four-flag, 18-wire resource (`spider_bc85.log`) with 14 output qubits in B/C code order. All seven target X generators and 128 orthogonal Z products were checked +1 after flag postselection (`spider_bc85_check.py`). A full bell2-source/square-tail assembly with real noise and abstract edges had raw/c1 pass and L=4.833282454749934e-5, A=0.7329848699119771, 20 clocks. Moving the precheck from B to C and scheduling the resource just in time gives 19 clocks and L=4.307575549077981e-5, A=0.7522068780402007; swapping the original A/B check order instead also gives 19 clocks but L=4.635153169372759e-5. Deleting the early B check worsens to 1.0228047543191106e-4. These controls isolate substantial accepted noisy residue beyond the clock delay and do not establish a native bound.

## Published low-overhead Steane checks

The repository `boldar99/Steane-Ultra-Low-Overhead` supplies the exact 14-CX nonadaptive instrument used here (see pinned URL and hash in `PROVENANCE.md`); code-coordinate permutation was derived by matching the full Steane stabilizer row spaces. `ulo_insert.py` appends its Z- or X-oriented check ahead of the complete seven-cat source's final square. All resets, gates, idles and measurements remain noisy, and the four output records are postselected through deterministic detectors. Both complete circuits pass official raw correctness and single-fault audit. Literal serial insertion has 31 clocks: Z loss 3.203327040715567e-5, A=0.6115840769296517; X loss 3.3494368229171975e-5, A=0.6116375708205626. CP-SAT co-scheduling of the Z instrument across source and resource operations produced `ulo_schedule/deep_005`, 25 clocks, 155 CX, raw-valid, L=2.9649858252424977e-5, A=0.640392636273766. It improves the prior abstract source but remains above the board key 2.86759593420732e-5 observed 2026-10-27 16:23:30 UTC; it is not a native candidate. The scheduler's 20-case loop was interrupted after 15 results for a more structural experiment.

`ulo_replace.py` removes the B/C terminal instrument and inserts one published syndrome check plus the retained A check. Both orientations are raw-correct but admit harmful singles (c1=0.4666667): full losses X=7.421871928765945e-4, Z=1.2976188184671072e-3 at 30 clocks. This is a bounded failed terminal replacement.

## Reused K block as encoded resource verifier

`deep_kverify.py` retains the full noisy seven-cat source/square ending and, after the second cat MX records, resets K into an encoded plus/zero block, couples it transversally to each candidate terminal resource, and measures it. It derives the full affine deterministic contract for the added records. On the staggered 2.97208e-5 parent, all three A/B/C checks pass raw/c1 but need five extra clocks; best B gives L=3.40840987968538e-5, A=0.5953166831. An exhaustive 7! relabeling cannot shorten the fixed encoding schedule.

On the different parent with simultaneous second-cat measurement, K is prepared immediately after the cat read and the transversal verification causes one extra clock. All three candidates pass raw/c1; best B is L=3.0391341135956134e-5, A=0.6497539903416338, 155 CX/22 clocks, versus its unverified parent L=3.035588264256369e-5. A/C give about 3.1957e-5. `deep_kverify_reuse_full.py` instead physically reuses measured K |±⟩ states for the encoded-plus inputs and recomputes the whole affine detector contract from scratch; best B remains raw/c1 correct at L=3.0447242161265563e-5, A=0.6488948219633996 and 22 clocks. The first attempt to carry old detectors unchanged was invalid; its failure is recorded in `deep_kverify_reuse_results.json`. These are finite constructions, not a proof against joint K/resource encoders or different source schedules.

A final bounded CP-SAT scheduling experiment on the one-clock B-verifier candidate completed 13 of 24 proposals before interruption; none improved its L=3.0391341135956134e-5 input. The included `deep_kverify_schedule_results.json` is the complete ledger of those 13 results, not a complete 24-case search. `publish_manifest.json` identifies the selected reproducible checkpoint, including pinned external provenance, and deliberately excludes the downloaded compiler source, binary dependencies and unselected QASM files.

## Output relocation and teleportation controls

The literal native-best role reversal was constructed from `work/lanes/SOTA_20261028/native_best/schedule_heralds_c167977fdd48de1f_d6_20_19_10_2_22_16`: reverse the final seven B→D CXs, measure old D in X, keep B as output, and apply the logical-Z frame from old-D parity. With the actual source unchanged, the first version has nondeterministic old-D syndrome rows and zero expectation for each output X stabilizer. `native_teleport_contract.py` rederives the entire deterministic affine detector space from scratch (79 rows): the output is still outside the code in 0.75 of accepted noiseless branches, with all three X-stabilizer expectations zero. A logical Pauli frame cannot repair physical code-stabilizer signs. The live source records c352/c353/c361 are tied to the retained output phase. This is a raw-validity obstruction to the literal fixed-source candidate, not a universal prohibition on teleportation.

A separate three-fresh-RX control changes those source operations, preserves the complete remaining native schedule and all physical noise, and passes raw correctness. Its exact phase-aware framed price is L=3.723759093407841e-5, A=0.49767282500321286, 412 CX: worse than the native reference and below the acceptance requirement. `native_teleport_results.json` and `native_teleport_contract.json` are the receipts.

`bell_terminal.py` tests encoded-Bell teleportation with C→D, Z measurement of D, X measurement of C, both logical Pauli frames, and six syndrome checks. The ideal input and Bell-resource preparation are explicitly made perfect, but the final CX, measurements and both receiver output idle clocks remain noisy. It passes raw/c1 and gives L=4.629582999571167e-5, A=0.980585462277909. This is a diagnostic, not a fully noisy implementation or a universal lower bound.

`onebit_terminal.py` tests the two encoded one-bit orientations under the same ideal-input/resource scope, with both receiver idles noisy. The X-frame orientation gives 4.5499160081401916e-5; the dual Z-frame orientation gives 3.4196229069739735e-5, both raw/c1 valid. `onebit_terminal_jit.py` suppresses only the receiver's first-check idle by assuming perfect just-in-time resource preparation; the final receiver CX and read-clock idle remain noisy. This counterfactual produces 2.8343208409416836e-5 (X frame) and 2.5576079222320233e-5 (Z frame), with A=0.9769351158341234. The latter is close to the ideal square-terminal reference, and shows that the earlier two-idle diagnostic does not exclude a better jointly prepared teleporting architecture. No physical perfect receiver preparation is claimed.

The actual complete abstract seven-cat source with explicitly noisy independent receiver/meter preparations is implemented in `onebit_full.py`: uncoupled X-frame and Z-frame versions pass raw and cost 5.677857544579224e-4 and 2.986875356753846e-4 respectively, 113 CX/20 clocks. Adding the literal transversal B→A resource coupling makes X raw-invalid and makes Z cost 5.837237484519923e-4, 120 CX/21 clocks. Thus neither simple noisy construction realizes the JIT counterfactual; correlated source/resource redesign remains untested rather than ruled out. All geometry is abstract for these controls.
